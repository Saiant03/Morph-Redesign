## 1. Webflow

### 1.1 GSAP acquisition / Interactions with GSAP
- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary, corroborated across primary-adjacent sources)**: Webflow acquired GreenSock (GSAP) in fall 2024. Source: https://gsap.com/blog/webflow-GSAP/ ("GSAP is Joining Webflow"), https://discourse.webflow.com/t/webflow-has-acquired-gsap/295180.
- **OBSERVED FACT**: On April 30, 2025 Webflow made the entire GSAP toolset — including all formerly Club GSAP-only plugins (SplitText, MorphSVG, ScrollSmoother, DrawSVG, Flip, etc.) — 100% free, including for commercial use, with no license key or membership required. Sources: Webflow's own blog post https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free and GSAP's own pricing page https://gsap.com/pricing/ ("The entire GSAP toolset is FREE, even for commercial use").
- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)**: Webflow shipped a rebuilt "Interactions" engine — "Webflow Interactions with GSAP" — powered by GSAP under the hood, available visually (no code) in the Designer: timeline-based editor, scroll triggers with pin/scrub, text-splitting for per-character stagger, animatable design variables, loop/repeat controls. This became the default interactions engine on new sites created after summer 2025; GSAP plugins (ScrollTrigger, SplitText) can be toggled per-site in settings. Sources: Webflow Help Center https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/42832301823635-Intro-to-Interactions-with-GSAP , https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/42861691922963-Glossary-for-Interactions-with-GSAP , Webflow blog https://webflow.com/blog/webflow-interactions-with-gsap .
- **INTERPRETATION**: This is the single most relevant 2025-2026 development for a "Webflow as concept/production tool" evaluation — Webflow now offers native, no-code access to real GSAP (ScrollTrigger-grade scroll choreography, SplitText character animation) rather than its older, more limited proprietary Interactions system. This substantially narrows the animation-capability gap between Webflow and a hand-coded GSAP build, at least for canvas-visible interactions; it does not give access to arbitrary custom GSAP timelines driven by app state/data (that still needs custom code embeds).

### 1.2 Webflow Ecommerce — plans and limits
- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary, from pricing aggregators quoting Webflow's May 2026 pricing update)**: Webflow eCommerce plans, billed yearly: **Standard** $29/mo (500 item limit, 2% transaction fee), **Plus** $74/mo (5,000 item limit, 0% transaction fee, positioned for stores up to ~$200k/yr revenue), **Advanced** $212/mo (15,000 item limit, all platform features, 0% fee). Source aggregating Webflow's own May 2026 pricing-update announcement: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/51059955082387-Updated-pricing-and-simplified-plans-for-May-2026 (official Webflow Help Center article, referenced by multiple secondary pricing write-ups such as https://www.joinamply.com/post/webflow-pricing and https://www.thecssagency.com/blog/webflow-pricing). Recommend verifying exact current numbers directly at https://webflow.com/pricing before client presentation, as Webflow revises pricing periodically (a May 2026 update is explicitly referenced).
- **INTERPRETATION**: For a perfume brand with a catalog in the low hundreds of SKUs (typical for a niche fragrance house), Webflow Ecommerce Standard/Plus item caps are not a binding constraint. The 2% transaction fee on Standard is a real cost if launched on the cheapest tier.

### 1.3 Where Webflow becomes limiting for commerce
**INTERPRETATION** (synthesizing pricing/limits above with general platform knowledge, since it wasn't independently re-verified page-by-page): Webflow Ecommerce lacks native multi-currency/multi-market pricing at the depth Shopify Markets offers, has a much smaller commerce app/plugin ecosystem than Shopify, and checkout customization is comparatively limited (no equivalent of Shopify's Checkout Extensibility UI-extension framework). It's strong for editorial/marketing pages and CMS-driven content, weaker as the transactional backbone at scale (loyalty points, complex promotions, POS/omnichannel, B2B) — all things the current morphparfum.ro WooCommerce stack already leans on via YITH plugins.

### 1.4 Motion implementation / commerce constraint on design / handoff
**INTERPRETATION**: Motion is authored visually in the Designer (Interactions with GSAP) — good for client handoff since a non-developer can inspect/tweak animations in the same tool used to edit layout, no separate codebase to maintain. Commerce templates (product/cart/checkout) in Webflow Ecommerce are still bound to Webflow's own template structure for those page types, constraining how much bespoke editorial layout can be applied to cart/checkout compared to marketing pages.

Cost indication: Webflow Ecommerce Standard $29/mo, Plus $74/mo, Advanced $212/mo (annual billing), per Webflow's May 2026 pricing update, verify at webflow.com/pricing — dated 2026-09-24 check.

Source: https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/plugins/framer-commerce/ , corroborated by https://www.managed-code.com/blog-post/webflow-vs-framer-ai-2026 ("Framer has no native e-commerce functionality").

Its code-component system means custom motion/3D (GSAP, R3F) can be embedded, but Framer is not built to own transactional flows, inventory, tax, or checkout — those still live on Shopify/other commerce backend behind Framer Commerce.

## 3. Shopify

### 3.1 Online Store 2.0 / Liquid / theme architecture
**INTERPRETATION** (general platform knowledge, not independently re-verified this session against shopify.dev in full): Online Store 2.0 (launched 2021, still current architecture in 2026) uses JSON templates + Liquid sections that can be added/reordered/configured by merchants without code, with app blocks letting third-party apps inject UI directly into theme sections. Liquid remains the storefront templating language for hosted (non-headless) themes.

### 3.2 Checkout Extensibility — the major 2025-2026 shift
- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary, multiple corroborating trade sources)**: Shopify is deprecating the old `checkout.liquid` customization model in favor of **Checkout Extensibility** — a framework of Checkout UI Extensions, Shopify Functions, a Branding API, and Web Pixels that each own a slice of what checkout.liquid used to control monolithically. Source: https://www.fudge.ai/blog/shopify-checkout-extensibility/ , https://ecosire.com/blog/shopify-checkout-extensibility .
- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)**: Plus-tier stores lost `checkout.liquid`, additional scripts, and script tags on Thank-you/Order-status pages on **August 28, 2025**, with automatic upgrades rolling out from January 2026. **Non-Plus stores have until August 26, 2026** to upgrade Thank-you/Order-status pages. Source: https://www.flatlineagency.com/blog/shopify-checkout-extensibility-2026/ , https://www.huptechweb.com/blogs/shopify-changelog-guide .
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Checkout UI Extensions can now query the **Storefront API** directly (e.g., customer/product tags) for custom checkout logic. Source: search-aggregated from https://ecosire.com/blog/shopify-checkout-extensibility and https://www.innovatrixinfotech.com/blog/shopify-checkout-extensibility-developer-guide.
- **INTERPRETATION**: This is highly relevant/time-sensitive for any Shopify recommendation dated "today" (2026-09-24): a non-Plus store built now must be built against Checkout Extensibility (UI Extensions/Functions), not legacy checkout.liquid, and the August 26, 2026 deadline has already passed as of this research date — meaning any live non-Plus Shopify store still on legacy checkout.liquid customization for Thank-you/Order-status pages should already be non-compliant/forcibly migrated. Worth flagging explicitly to the client if Shopify is chosen.

### 3.3 Markets / Romanian Lei (RON) support
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Shopify's official Help Center documents Shopify Payments availability for Romania (accepting cards, accelerated checkout, and local payment methods). Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/romania .
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Shopify's official docs for **Markets** cover multi-currency/multi-language localization, including displaying prices in local currency, price rounding, and per-market price percentage adjustments. Sources: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/getting-started/localization , https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/pricing , https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/markets/multiple-currencies-languages .
- **INTERPRETATION**: RON as the store's home currency is fully supported (Romania has an official Shopify Payments country listing), so a Shopify concept for morphparfum.ro can run natively in RON with Markets used later if EU-wide expansion is desired (e.g., EUR pricing for other markets) — this is a genuine advantage over the current WooCommerce/Stripe setup, which likely requires a currency-switcher plugin for the same effect.

### 3.4 Storefront API / headless
**INTERPRETATION** (general knowledge, consistent with search results on Hydrogen below): Shopify's Storefront API (GraphQL) is the standard interface for headless storefronts (Hydrogen or any custom frontend), and now also usable from within Checkout UI Extensions per 3.2 above. The Cart API (part of Storefront API) manages cart sessions and produces a `checkoutUrl` that hands off to Shopify's hosted checkout — meaning even a fully custom Next.js/React frontend against Shopify still terminates at Shopify's own checkout unless deeper Function/Extension customization is used. Source basis: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/liquid/objects/checkout and the checkout-extensibility sources in 3.2.

Cost indication: Shopify's plan pricing (Basic/Grow/Advanced/Plus) was not independently re-fetched this session; recommend confirming current tiers/prices at https://www.shopify.com/pricing before quoting.

## 4. Shopify Hydrogen / Oxygen (headless)

- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary, from Shopify ecosystem trade coverage citing Hydrogen's own changelog/updates page)**: Hydrogen migrated its routing/data layer from **Remix v2 to React Router v7** in late 2024 — Shopify took React Router's routing/loaders/actions primitives (without full Remix framework opinions) and wrapped them with Hydrogen-specific abstractions (Oxygen edge runtime, Storefront API client, cart management, analytics, customer accounts, B2B). Source: https://weaverse.io/blogs/shopify-hydrogen (citing Hydrogen's official updates page https://hydrogen.shopify.dev/updates), https://weaverse.io/blogs/remix-3-beta-hydrogen-react-router-7-2026 .
- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)**: Hydrogen **2026.4** (dated "shipped ... on April 17" per source) introduced three breaking changes: (1) mandatory Storefront API proxy, (2) backend consent mode replacing `_tracking_consent`, and (3) deprecation of the `@shopify/remix-oxygen` package — its exports move to `react-router` directly and to `@shopify/hydrogen`/`@shopify/hydrogen/oxygen`. Source: https://weaverse.io/blogs/hydrogen-2026-4-breaking-changes-storefront-proxy-consent-mode , https://www.ontapgroup.com/blog/shopify-hydrogen-v202640 .

For a small/mid brand agency without dedicated ongoing engineering resources, this churn is a real maintenance-cost consideration versus a Shopify Online Store 2.0 theme (Liquid) or a lower-churn stack.

## 5. Other headless options (brief)

- **Medusa**: **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)** — TypeScript-based, open-source, API-first headless commerce; described as having "the largest community and fastest release cadence" among open-source commerce backends in 2026, well suited to small/medium projects, pairs naturally with Next.js/React (shared TypeScript mental model). Source: https://vendure.io/blog/best-headless-commerce-platforms , https://www.linearloop.io/blog/medusa-js-vs-saleor-vs-vendure , https://kanopylabs.com/blog/medusa-vs-saleor-vs-vendure-open-source-commerce (33.4% month-on-month growth figure cited vs. Saleor's 2.1%, secondary/unverified-primary).
- **Saleor**: **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)** — Open-source, GraphQL-first, built on Python/Django, BSD-3 licensed, multi-channel commerce as first-class core feature; longest production track record (project started 2012) and most enterprise deployments among the open-source set, but heavier to self-host/operate (needs Python + GraphQL expertise, dedicated DevOps). Source: https://www.netguru.com/blog/saleor-vs-medusa , https://www.linearloop.io/blog/medusa-js-vs-saleor-vs-vendure .
- **WooCommerce headless**: **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)** — Achievable via WooCommerce's REST API and the newer Store API (part of WooCommerce Blocks, designed for headless cart use), paired with a Next.js frontend using React Server Components/App Router for performance; cart is typically managed client-side or via the Store API, with order creation at checkout time. No official first-party "headless Woo" product from Automattic was found — implementations are community/agency-built. Source: https://blazecommerce.io/blog/headless-woocommerce-with-next-js/ , https://www.creaytic.com/blog/woocommerce-vs-nextjs-commerce-2026-architecture-guide .
- **INTERPRETATION**: Since morphparfum.ro already runs WooCommerce, a "headless WooCommerce + Next.js" path is technically the lowest-migration-risk headless option (keep existing backend/catalog/orders, replace only the frontend) — but it inherits WooCommerce's comparatively thinner headless tooling (no first-party Storefront-API equivalent to Shopify's) and would require bespoke cart/checkout engineering. Medusa is the better-fit *green-field* open-source option for a small/mid brand wanting full control without Shopify's SaaS constraints, given its React/TypeScript alignment and faster-moving ecosystem; Saleor is over-engineered for this brand's likely scale.

## 9. Lenis

- **OBSERVED FACT**: Lenis ("smooth" in Latin) is a lightweight smooth-scroll library built by **darkroom.engineering** (rebranded from Studio Freight). The old `@studio-freight/lenis` and `@studio-freight/react-lenis`/`@studio-freight/hamo` packages were retired; the current package is published simply as `lenis` on npm, with docs/repo at the darkroom.engineering GitHub org. Source: https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis (official repo), https://www.npmjs.com/package/@studio-freight/lenis (legacy package, redirect note).
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Lenis works by intercepting wheel/touch events and driving scroll position via linear interpolation (lerp), commonly paired with GSAP ScrollTrigger and WebGL scroll-synced scenes (parallax, R3F canvases). Source: darkroom.engineering GitHub repo README (per search-result summary), corroborated by https://devdreaming.com/blogs/nextjs-smooth-scrolling-with-lenis-gsap .
- **INTERPRETATION**: Lenis is the current de facto standard for "buttery" smooth-scroll in award-site-style builds (confirmed as part of the stack in the Cartier Watches & Wonders case study, §11) and integrates directly with GSAP ScrollTrigger — this pairing (Lenis + GSAP + Next.js/React) is the dominant technical pattern for the kind of motion-rich experience implied by the brief.

## 10. Motion (formerly Framer Motion)

- **OBSERVED FACT**: The library formerly named "Framer Motion" is now published simply as **Motion** (motion.dev), having become an independent project (decoupled from Framer the company) in 2025, keeping the same core team and API but a leaner package. Source: https://motion.dev/ (official site), corroborated by https://dev.to/stacknotice/framer-motion-motion-react-animations-complete-guide-2026-3e7l .
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Motion's hybrid engine runs animations natively via the Web Animations API and ScrollTimeline where possible (targeting 120fps), falling back to JavaScript-driven animation when needed; the React build is roughly 31KB gzipped and includes layout animations, `AnimatePresence` for exit transitions, gesture support, and scroll-driven animation hooks. Source: https://motion.dev/docs/react (official docs).
- **INTERPRETATION**: Motion is the React-idiomatic alternative/complement to GSAP — better suited to component-driven, state-based UI animation (page/route transitions, `AnimatePresence`, layout animations tied to React state) whereas GSAP+ScrollTrigger remains stronger for free-form scroll choreography and timeline sequencing independent of component re-renders. A production build for morphparfum.ro would likely use both: GSAP/ScrollTrigger/Lenis for editorial scroll storytelling, Motion for UI-level micro-interactions (filters, cart drawer, page transitions) if built on Next.js/React.

## 12. View Transitions API

- **OBSERVED FACT**: Same-document View Transitions ship in Chromium from Chrome 111, WebKit/Safari from Safari 18, and Gecko/Firefox from Firefox 144. Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API (MDN), corroborated by https://caniuse.com/view-transitions .
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Cross-document (multi-page, MPA-style) View Transitions are supported in Chromium browsers (Chrome 126+) and Safari 18.2+, but **not** in any stable Firefox release as of this research date; Firefox 144 only closed the gap for same-document transitions. Source: https://brainstormsandraves.com/css/view-transitions-2026/ , cross-referenced with MDN's Baseline status note.
- **OBSERVED FACT**: MDN's own compatibility annotation explicitly marks the View Transitions API as "Limited availability... not Baseline, because it does not work in some of the most widely-used browsers" (i.e., Firefox for cross-document). Source: MDN View Transition API page (per search-result summary), https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API .
- **INTERPRETATION**: Native View Transitions are viable as a *progressive enhancement* for same-document (SPA-style, e.g. Next.js client-side route changes) transitions today, since Chrome/Edge/Safari cover the large majority of traffic and the API degrades gracefully (no transition, normal navigation) in Firefox. Cross-document/MPA view transitions are riskier to rely on as the *only* transition mechanism given Firefox's non-support; a GSAP- or Motion-driven transition layer remains the safer choice if cross-browser consistency of the transition itself (not just functional navigation) matters to the brand experience.

## 13. AI-assisted builders — realistic role

- **OBSERVED FACT (secondary)**: Three tools dominate the AI web-builder conversation in 2026 — **v0** (Vercel), **Lovable**, **Bolt.new** — each turning natural-language prompts into working code, but solving different problems: v0 (rebranded v0.dev → **v0.app** in January 2026) is positioned as full-stack-adjacent but its core strength/actual output remains **frontend-only** — production-quality React/Next.js code using shadcn/ui + Tailwind, with **no backend capability**. Lovable generates exportable React code with built-in Supabase auth/DB and one-click deploy/GitHub export (cited "$20M ARR in 2 months," an unverified growth claim from a secondary/marketing-adjacent source, flagged as low-confidence). Bolt.new pairs a full in-browser IDE with AI chat and Supabase backend integration. Source: https://uibakery.io/blog/bolt-vs-lovable-vs-v0 , https://blog.tooljet.com/lovable-vs-bolt-vs-v0/ , https://lovable.dev/guides/best-ai-app-builders (Lovable's own comparison page — self-interested source, read with appropriate skepticism).
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Webflow's AI Site Builder generates starter pages/sections/placeholder copy/basic styling as editable native Webflow elements; per Webflow's own product positioning it explicitly **cannot** create new CMS collections (only add/update items in existing ones) and **cannot create or apply Webflow Interactions** — animation still has to be built manually even when AI generates the layout. Source: https://www.thecssagency.com/blog/webflow-ai-site-builder (citing Webflow's stated AI feature scope), consistent with general Webflow AI product documentation.
- **OBSERVED FACT**: Framer ships "Framer Agents"/"Wireframer" — AI that can design sections, revise copy, review SEO, and organize CMS content; Wireframer generates a responsive page draft in under a minute. Framer has no platform-level AEO (answer-engine-optimization) tooling, unlike Webflow which has shipped sitewide AEO auditing and AI schema/meta generation plus enterprise AEO tooling and a native Claude connector. Source: https://www.managed-code.com/blog-post/webflow-vs-framer-ai-2026 .
- **INTERPRETATION**: For this project, AI builders (v0/Lovable/Bolt) are realistically useful as **rapid concept/prototype scaffolding tools** — generating throwaway or lightly-adapted component code, layout drafts, or interaction demos to validate direction fast — not as the production build system for a brand-critical, motion-choreographed commerce site. Every source characterizing "AI-built output" in 2026 (including the "why every AI site looks the same" critique found in search results, https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/blog/ai-built-website-no-slop.html) frames it as a starting point requiring substantial manual design/engineering refinement — directly echoing Webflow's and Framer's own stated limitations for their in-canvas AI tools. None of these tools currently own bespoke, hand-tuned GSAP/Lenis/R3F choreography, which is precisely the differentiator an "award-level" editorial commerce site needs.
