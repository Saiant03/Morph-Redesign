# 07 — Preliminary creative directions

Three genuinely different territories, each built on Morph's own product truths (05). **No direction is chosen yet.** Typeface names are candidates to test, and licensing is unchecked. Colors are directional, not final tokens.

Shared, non-negotiable in all three:
- The juice colors are real data, taken from the bottles.
- The core store stays fast and conventional (PLP, filters, cart, checkout).
- Every signature moment has a touch-native form and a reduced-motion variant.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for text on any color field. This is checked per scent, and text flips between light and dark.

---

## A. CROMATIC — the synesthetic atlas

**Core idea.** Morph's founding idea is *scent = color = emotion*. The site is a calm gallery in which each fragrance **is** its color. Browsing the catalog is moving through a color atlas; families are color neighbourhoods.

**Emotional character.** Clear, confident, curious, and luminous rather than dark and mysterious.

**Visual language.** Large flat fields of juice color, with the bottle photographed in true color on a matching field. There are no gradients or textures: the color itself is the material. The UI chrome is almost absent.

**Typography.** One sans-serif family with a wide weight range, set large and quiet. Candidates: *Neue Montreal*, *Söhne*, *ABC Favorit*. The serif logo stays the only serif. Type never competes with the color field.

**Color.** A neutral that is neither cream nor black: cool paper white / soft mineral grey (≈#EDEEEC / #1D1E1F for text). The accent palette is the full set of ~25 juice colors; one leads per screen. Families are defined as color ranges (gourmand = ambers, fresh = aquas and greens, …).

**Imagery.** New packshots on color-matched seamless paper (one shoot session, repeatable); ingredient macro shots in the same hue; boutique photography in daylight.

**Layout.** A gallery grid with generous margins. PDPs are split: a full-bleed color plane with the bottle on one side, information on the other. The homepage is an index of colors (a "color library" of all scents) plus the collections as three chapters.

**Motion.** Background color interpolates between scents as you browse (hover or swipe) and on route change. Notes over time appear as a color shift from top-note to base-note hue. Everything else is still.

**Interaction.**
- "Color index" navigation: scrub a horizontal strip of all scents.
- The layering composer blends two fields into a third.
- Filters recolour the grid.

**Product presentation.** The bottle is always centered on its own color. It is the most product-true of the three directions.

**Why it fits Morph.** It turns the founding claim (synesthesia) and the most visible product feature (colored juices) into the system itself. It is instantly recognizable, and no reference brand does it (most use clear glass and neutral UIs).

**Risks.**
- Contrast management on ~25 backgrounds.
- The site could read as "colorful DTC" instead of niche luxury; restraint in type and space is essential.
- A new, consistent photo shoot is required.
- Juice colors may be too similar within a family.

**Why it could become a distinctive identity.** A visitor could recognize Morph from a single cropped screenshot: a bottle on its own color with quiet type. The system scales to new launches for free (each new scent brings its own color).

## B. FORMA — the twist

**Core idea.** *Morphē* means form. The Bormioli bottle's twist ("echilibru și mișcare continuă") becomes the geometry of the whole site: a grid sheared by the bottle's twist angle, modules that rotate slightly out of axis, glass refraction as the one visual effect.

**Emotional character.** Sculptural, architectural, avant-garde and a little severe: design object over beauty product.

**Visual language.**
- Structure is expressed through geometry: offset columns, sheared image masks, overlapping planes that return to alignment on interaction ("balance restored").
- Imagery is mostly monochrome (glass, metal cap, shadow). **Color appears only inside the glass**, as light passing through the juice.

**Typography.**
- An expressive variable grotesk with a **width axis**, compressing and expanding with the twist. Candidates: *Anybody*, *Roboto Flex*-class width families, or a licensed display face such as *Monument Grotesk* variable.
- A plain text face for body copy.
- Type is used as form: large compressed words cropped by the grid.

**Color.** Cool concrete grey, graphite and mercury silver (the bottle cap), clear white. Juice color is confined to glass and light. This is not near-black luxury: the base is mid-grey concrete, not black.

**Imagery.** A studio shoot of the bottle as sculpture: hard light, caustics (colored light cast through the juice onto surfaces), extreme close-ups of the twist. Few people.

**Layout.** Asymmetric and editorial, with a sheared module system. Commerce pages (PLP, cart) return to a strict grid, which shows the principle: tension in the story, balance in the shop.

**Motion.** Shear and rotate transitions that resolve to straight on arrival. One WebGL moment (hero): real-time refraction or caustics of the bottle. SplitText width animations on headings, sparingly.

**Interaction.**
- The cursor or a tilt gently twists the hero bottle.
- The PDP gallery rotates the bottle around the twist axis (a photo sequence, not necessarily 3D).
- In the layering composer, two bottles cast overlapping caustics.

**Product presentation.** The bottle is shown as a design object, with product data set in an architectural spec style.

**Why it fits Morph.** It is built from the most distinctive physical asset (the bottle form) and from the brand name. It separates Morph from soft, romantic fragrance aesthetics.

**Risks.**
- The highest craft and performance cost (WebGL and a caustics shoot).
- Readability and usability risk if the geometry leaks into shopping pages.
- It could feel cold for a scent brand, and it depends heavily on photography.
- The most experimental of the three, so the hardest to hand off.

**Why it could become a distinctive identity.** A "twist" geometry is a brand-specific motion and layout signature that competitors cannot adopt without looking like Morph.

## C. STRATA — your next form

**Core idea.** Identity is built **layer by layer**. The interface is made of translucent layers (veils, mists, fabric) that combine. Scroll is time on skin: the page itself evolves from top notes to base notes. Layering is the central interaction, not a sub-page.

**Emotional character.** Intimate, sensual and transformative: the most emotional and immersive of the three.

**Visual language.**
- Overlapping semi-opaque planes. Images sit behind frosted or veiled layers that clear as you advance.
- **Blends computed from real pairs of juice colors**, never decorative gradients.
- Skin, fabric and mist textures.

**Typography.**
- A refined humanist or contrast sans for text. Candidates: *Instrument Sans* or *Inter Display*-class faces; avoid the generic look through a custom setting.
- A light italic cut used as the second "layer" voice, where it overlaps or echoes the first in two sizes. Headlines literally layer two lines of type (a statement and its "next form").

**Color.** Near-white mist, skin-warm neutrals and deep aubergine-brown as the dark end. Pairing colors appear only as blends between two real juice colors.

**Imagery.**
- Close-ups of spray mist, skin, draped fabric, hands (continuing the existing Your Next Form campaign, which already uses hands and the box).
- Short silent loops.

**Layout.**
- Long-form, chaptered scroll on brand and PDP pages: *spray → heart → trail*.
- A sticky buy bar keeps shopping one tap away.
- A card-free grid on the PLP, with images and text on layered planes.

**Motion.**
- Dissolves and veils clear as content is reached.
- Time-scrub on the PDP (0 min → 10 h).
- The composer blends two veils into a third.
- Page transitions dissolve rather than slide.

**Interaction.**
- The layering composer is the homepage centerpiece.
- The blind reveal page (QR in the box) is a staged unveil.
- "Your next form" saves a user's pairings to their account.

**Product presentation.** The scent is presented as an experience over time. The bottle appears after the mood, not before it (with the buy bar always visible).

**Why it fits Morph.** It carries the current campaign ("Your Next Form — identity, layer by layer") into the whole experience, makes the blind layering line the core of the brand, and gives transformation a behavioral meaning, not an illustration.

**Risks.**
- Translucency and blur can hurt contrast and performance (backdrop-filter on mobile).
- It is closest to generic "dreamy beauty" aesthetics if executed loosely.
- Long scroll can slow fast shoppers; the sticky buy bar and a "skip to details" link are mandatory.
- Depends on new mist and skin imagery.

**Why it could become a distinctive identity.** No fragrance brand makes layering its structural principle. Owning "layer by layer" as both campaign and interface gives Morph a concept competitors would need a product line to copy.

## How they differ

| | A. Cromatic | B. Forma | C. Strata |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source truth | Juice color, synesthesia | Bottle twist, *morphē* | Layering line, scent over time |
| Temperature | Luminous, clear | Cool, sculptural | Warm, intimate |
| Base | Mineral white | Concrete grey | Mist / skin neutrals |
| Type role | Quiet | Type as form | Layered voices |
| Signature | Color index + color blend | Refraction hero + twist transitions | Composer + time-scrub + reveal |
| Build risk | Low–medium | High | Medium |
| Photo need | Color-matched packshots | Sculptural studio, caustics | Mist, skin, fabric, hands |
| Most "experimental" axis | Color system | Geometry and WebGL | Immersion and interaction |

Hybrid note (for Phase 02, not a decision): A's color data and C's layering behavior are compatible; B is the most self-contained.

## How the choice will be made (Phase 02)

For each direction, build the same three test screens (Home first viewport, PDP on mobile, layering composer) with real data. Then compare them on brand fit, clarity for a first-time visitor, mobile usability, contrast and performance budgets, and production cost.
